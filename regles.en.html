1. In brief

Colonise the solar system, develop your technologies, accumulate victory points (VP). The game lasts 10 turns; the most VP wins. Each turn you spend Action Points (AP); the colonies connected to your base produce at the end of the turn.

These penalties are calculated on the morale you have at the moment you end your turn, not on what is left once the wars are resolved. A drop caused by one or more wars therefore only penalises you at the end of the following turn: you have a whole turn to fix it.

The planets (Mercury to Neptune) are scenery: neither colonisable nor attackable. Play takes place on their moons, on the asteroids and on the dwarf planets. The board shows the Sun at the bottom left and each body at its real distance (logarithmic scale); the moons are drawn in an inset around their planet, without respecting distance, only order.

The possible links are the blue dotted lines on the map (30 in all). A route can only be built on a link; the travel time (in days) is written on the long links and is used for the cost of conquests (§14). Since v10.70: Vesta–Europa, Moon–Vesta, Ceres–Ganymede and Europa–Callisto no longer exist; Ceres–Europa (58 d), Europa–Ganymede (4 d), Enceladus–Vesta (125 d), Callisto–Enceladus (78 d) and Ganymede–Triton (190 d) do.

6.1 Upgrading

Level 2: income ×1.5, +1❤️/turn, +1🔬/turn.
Level 3: income ×2, +2❤️/turn, +2🔬/turn.

The ×1.5 is rounded down: a colony producing 1⚡ still produces 1 at level 2. The yield of all three levels is shown on each node of the map — look at it before colonising.

6.2 Routes

Upkeep 1⚡/turn, offset by 1🪨 of trade income. A route stays connected as long as it exists; the token placed on it serves only to protect it from pirates.

A route only counts if it is yours: both its ends are your colonies (capital and cohabitation included), or one is yours and the other is free. A route to another nation's colony yields neither 🪨 nor victory points (nor for the Master of Routes agenda) — it still costs its upkeep.

A route with no end of yours falls. At the start of each turn, any route neither end of which is one of your colonies is abandoned and its token comes back to you — for instance when you lose the colony that held it. A route with only one end of yours holds.

6.3 Raid

You take the production of a colony — its income for the turn in ⚡, 🪨 and 🔬, never its ❤️. You receive it at once; the raided nation does not collect it at the end of the turn (its stock is untouched: it is that colony's income that goes missing). A raid always targets one specific colony, which you choose. A colony can only be raided once per turn, by anyone: the Raid button says so (“already raided this turn”). Your tokens go into recovery for 2 turns (1 with War Strategy). Tension: +3 victim, +1 raider. The raid does not touch the victim's morale — tension (demonstrations, popular war) does the rest.

🔍 Defensive AI cancels the theft, 🛰️ Surveillance Drones remove 1 unit from each resource stolen. A blocked raid still costs AP and tokens, gives +1 tension, and the raider is told about it.

6.4 Trade agreement

  • Tension −3 on both sides. While it holds: +1🪨 +1❤️/turn for each — per agreement, not per colony.
  • Your routes on its colonies no longer create tension, and you can cross its territory to connect more distant colonies of yours without risking war. Colonising its nodes remains forbidden.
  • One agreement per pair. If both propose it to each other at the same summit, only one is signed — and both are told.
  • Broken by a declaration of war or an assault, not by a raid. (What happens to routes in war is described in 14.4; an assault alone breaks no route.)

6.5 Cohabitation

Only Extra-Solar Exploration lets two nations occupy the same node, on Eris, Pluto or Triton.

  • The original occupant keeps its colony intact.
  • The newcomer receives a level 1, non-upgradable colony, with a forced trade agreement on that node. It counts fully for VP and agendas.
  • A cohabitant may assault the other: the winner stays alone, its colony takes the level of the conquered one minus one, the upgrade ban and the forced agreement fall away.
  • Except under a non-aggression pact: as long as a diplomatic pact binds the two cohabitants, neither can assault the other on the shared node. A mere trade agreement does not protect: the assault remains possible (it breaks the agreement).
  • Against an outside nation, the two cohabitants defend together: the original owner chooses its tokens first, then the guest is consulted in turn; both defences add up to the garrison. Each pays what it commits. If the attacker wins, it expels both (and collects the +10 VP if it is a capital).
  • If the shared node is the capital of one of the two, its owner defends it first (garrison of 10); the guest is warned, then consulted after it.
  • A war between the two cohabitants drives the guest off the shared node; the original owner keeps its colony.

12.4 The grudge — what a nation does not forget

Tension fades: it comes down on its own, a peace halves it, a pact resets it to zero. The grudge, however, does not fade with time. Every raid and every assault you inflict on a nation — successful or not — is added to its counter, and that counter hardens all its answers to your requests: trade agreement, non-aggression pact, peace offer.

Concretely, when it decides, it feels your tension +1 from the first aggression and +2 from the third onwards. An agreement that would have passed at 6 tension is refused if you have already attacked it, and the refusal tells you why. At low tension, on the other hand, a grudge alone blocks nothing: a nation at peace stays open.

And reconciliation is always possible. A 🕊️ Diplomacy mission aimed at that nation wipes its slate clean in one go — it does not depend on its consent, so blocked diplomacy can always be unblocked. A signed non-aggression pact wipes it too, on both sides.

13. Pirates

The pirates live in the main asteroid belt (Ceres, Vesta) and in the Kuiper belt (Pluto, Eris). They plunder the routes of every nation — except those of the Belters, to whom they sell their contraband.

Which routes? Those with one end 60 days of travel or less from a den (Phobos, Deimos, Moon, Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto, Triton and the dens themselves). A route with both ends further out (Titan–Enceladus, for example) is never visited.

When and how much? At the start of each turn, after the Strategy cards are chosen, each route in range is plundered with a 70% chance if it is unprotected, 30% if it carries a Force token (another nation's token on the same link is enough). Several routes can be plundered in the same turn. Each plunder steals 1 resource drawn at random among ⚡, 🪨 and 🔬 — if you have none of that one, another you own. The resource is lost (it does not go to the Belters). The route does not fall. The plundered nation is warned, route by route.

Tension. A plundered nation sees its tension towards the Belters rise by 1 per turn, whatever the number of routes hit. Spared nations do not change.

🔍 Orbital Network, 🔍 Defensive AI and 🔮 Empath Links make you immune — on purchase, the tokens placed on your routes come back for free.

14. War

14.1 How it starts

An assault is not a declaration of war. It is a surprise strike — the attacker gives no warning. The assaulted nation learns of it when it comes to resist: it chooses its tokens (and its Supercruiser) and the battle is resolved at once. But no war opens: what the aggression leaves behind is the tension of the nation attacked.

Tension is the only road to war. Each captured colony adds +8 to the attacked nation's tension, each repelled assault +6 — a failed attack is not forgiven. It adds up, up to 10: a second captured colony therefore almost always leads to war. The attacker adds nothing to its own. At 10, at the end of the turn, the people demand war: that is the forced popular war, and it is the only door — along with refusing a Dyson Sphere and unmasked espionage. At that moment you can still buy or propose peace; the window tells you what the war will cost you.

Whoever wants war at any price therefore still gets it: it is enough to attack as long as actions remain. It simply does not arrive in the middle of the turn in which you have just lost morale by losing a colony.

Same sequence whether the assault comes from a player or from the computer. An assault breaks the trade agreement that bound the two nations — you do not trade with those you attack — but it breaks neither routes nor cohabitation colonies: that only happens on the declaration of a real war.

14.2 Battle

Everything is played in Force tokens. Each token committed costs 1🪨 + 1⚡: you can only commit what you can pay for (Navigation AI halves that cost). The Empaths bonus (+2 or +4) is added for free. The Supercruiser (+5), however, is paid for each time it is deployed: 5🪨 + 5⚡ (2🪨 + 3⚡ with Navigation AI), see §14.7.

  • The defender chooses its defence. Without defence, the attacker takes the target without spending anything: committing tokens is the only way to make it lose forces.
  • Your assaults are not capped — only your AP, your tokens and your resources limit them. A computer-controlled nation only assaults you once per turn.
  • Hold position: you may keep your tokens; if both sides hold, nothing happens. As long as the battle is not resolved, you can step back without spending anything.

14.3 The order of battles at the end of the turn

Once war is open, the first turn belongs to whoever opened it: only they strike. From the following turn, each side leads an assault and suffers the other's — two battles at the end of the turn. Since each token has to be paid for, tokens and resources must be split between attack and defence: that is where the war is decided.

The initiative does not say who strikes first, but who chooses which of the two strikes first. It is decided by the first rule that separates them:

#CriterionWho wins it
1🌀 HyperdriveIts holder. If both have it, the powers cancel out and we move on to the next criterion.
2RestraintWhoever assaulted the other the least during the action phase.

Defending first means knowing what you have left before choosing your assault. Attacking first means striking while you still have your tokens. Neither is better: it all depends on what the opponent can still pay. The log says each time who holds the initiative and why.

14.4 Reserve and garrison

Each connected colony other than your base ties up 1 token in reserve: your attack-capable tokens are your total tokens minus your connected colonies. The more you expand, the less you can attack.

Every colony adds 1 garrison token to the defence committed: none is defenceless. If it falls, that token is destroyed.

The capital. Held by its original nation, it can be attacked but is defended by 10 garrison tokens. Taking it from its original nation yields +10 VP — as long as you hold it: if it escapes its conqueror (retaken by its owner, or taken by a third party), those 10 VP vanish at once; the third party gets nothing. Once conquered, it defends itself like an ordinary colony (1 token): its original nation can take it back — without any VP bonus. Once retaken, its garrison starts again at 1 and gains +1 per turn up to 10; all players are told at the moment it is retaken.

Routes in war. On the declaration of war, any route neither end of which is yours and one end of which belongs to the enemy is broken and its token comes back to you.

The defence window announces the garrison of the place attacked (10 capital, 1 colony, retaken capital: its current value) and the total of your defence.

A nation with no colony at all no longer plays: it is over for it until the end of the game (it keeps its cards, its tokens and its points, but no longer acts and can no longer be attacked). A nation that has lost its capital but keeps other colonies goes on playing.

Demanding peace (popular war). When your people force war on you, you can demand peace against a tribute of 3 — taken first from the opponent's 🪨, topped up in ⚡ if it does not have enough (1🪨 left → 1🪨 + 2⚡). A nation stronger than you in tokens refuses, and a nation that cannot gather 3 in total cannot buy peace: in both cases the war goes on, with no battle that turn. A human opponent receives the question and may refuse; the amount is recalculated at the moment it accepts.

14.6 Attacking a route

Attacking a route is a choice of the end-of-turn war window — for everyone, computers included: nobody destroys a route during its action phase. A single assault is enough, and a route does not defend itself. 1 token if it is unprotected, 2 if it is protected — the defending token is then destroyed. Then you take it over (it becomes yours, upkeep included) or you destroy it; in both cases the opponent loses its income. No VP: taking or destroying a route is not a battle victory.

14.7 Supercruiser

+5 power in war — nothing against raids or pirates. The game asks at each battle whether you deploy it; deployment costs 5🪨 + 5⚡. Never lost: in case of defeat, 2 turns of repairs (1 with War Strategy) — the game tells you at the moment of the battle, with the turn of return. Usable once in attack and once in defence in the same turn, if you can pay twice.

14.8 Intelligence

Without 🔍 Orbital Network: estimate to ±3 of the rivals' strength, economy and morale hidden, enemy defence unknown at the moment of committing. Colonies, routes, VP and tensions remain public — including tensions between two other nations, visible in the Diplomacy tab and in the “ease a tension” window (“towards / from” format). Computer-controlled nations suffer the same fog and therefore get it wrong too.

18. Sequence of a turn

  1. Announcement of the event, if there is one.
  2. Strategy cards, from the weakest nation to the strongest.
  3. Pirate plunder of the routes near the belts (see 13).
  4. Action phase: each spends its AP. The order is drawn at random each turn — except for whoever played Initiative or Planned Initiative (see 9).
  5. Tension update.
  6. Battles, if a war is in progress.
  7. Income, then upkeep.
  8. Event resolution.
  9. On turns 2 and 6: choice of an investment.
